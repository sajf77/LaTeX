
\chapter*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Introduction}
The study of analytic functions, particularly univalent and multivalent functions, has been a central topic in complex analysis due to their rich geometric and functional properties. Univalent functions, which are injective in the unit disk, and their subclasses, such as starlike, convex, and close-to-convex functions, have been extensively studied for their coefficient bounds, growth, and distortion properties.  

Multivalent functions, or $p$-valent functions, generalize univalent functions by allowing exactly $p$ pre-images for each point in their range, leading to a broader spectrum of function behavior. Investigating coefficient bounds for these functions provides crucial information about their geometric structure, stability, and mapping properties. Bi-univalent functions, a subclass where both the function and its inverse are univalent, have gained significant attention due to their intricate coefficient relationships and applications in geometric function theory.  

This research focuses on establishing coefficient bounds for various families of holomorphic multivalent functions. Using tools such as differential subordination and the convolution of analytic functions, we derive new inequalities for $p$-valent starlike, convex, close-to-convex, and bi-univalent functions. These results not only extend classical coefficient estimates but also provide a foundation for further exploration in multivalent function theory and related mathematical fields.
